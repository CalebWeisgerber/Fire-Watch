SEATTLE KIRO BUILDATHON • YOUR CHALLENGE 
Where does Washington burn? 
Every year, Washington records thousands of wildfires: a lightning strike in the Cascades, an escaped debris burn in Klickitat, a late-summer tire in Okanogan. Each one is logged with where it started, how many acres it burned, what caused it, and the dates A was discovered, controlled, and put out. It all lands in one open dataset, covering the whole state. Your challenge: imagine a public official who works with wildfire, then build the one thing that official most needs from this data. Someone who plans where to reduce fire risk. Someone who coordinates before and during a season. Someone accountable for how a region prepares and responds. Those are sparks, not a menu, so picture your own official and your own angle. There's no single right answer. A strong build names a specific official, solves a real problem for them, and stands on real wildfire data. Get data on screen early, find the one thing that makes your app useful to the official you pictured, and grow it from there. How to approach it (a way to move, not a recipe, steps 2 and 3 are yours) 1. Look at the data first. Open the wildfire data and read a few real rows. Notice what's there: fire name, county, acres burned, cause, discovery date, location. Your best ideas come from what's actually in there, not what you assume is there. 2. Pick your official. Who is this for? Someone who plans where to reduce fire risk, someone who coordinates a season's response, someone accountable for how a region prepares. Picture the kind of public official whose job this data would land on. Naming a specific person is what turns a data dump into a tool. 3. Find the real problem. What does that official actually need from this data to do their job? Not "show wildfires," but the specific question they'd want answered. This is your idea. Protect it. 4. Get a quick win. In your own words, ask Kiro to pull real wildfire data and get it on screen. Seeing real rows render is your momentum, so chase that first, before anything fancy. 5. Decide what makes it useful. Find the one view, filter, or interaction that turns "data on a page" into "something that official would actually open at work." That one thing is your app. 6. iterate. Keep directing Kiro to add and improve: more function, a cleaner interface, the theme and tone that fit your user. With only -2 hours, you won't get to everything on your wish list, and that's expected. Make it a little better, then a little better again. 7. If you're tapped to demo, prep your story. Not every team presents. If yours is picked, get your story straight: who it's for, the one problem it solves, why it fits that official, and the single thing you'll show first. A clear walk-through of the working app is what lands, so lead with the thing that works. And have a short answer ready for "what would you build next?" Two i hours is never enough, so naming the features you'd add shows you understand your user beyond what you had time to ship. A few directions teams have taken (yours doesn't have to be here) 
• Map fires across a region an official is responsible for. • Break fires down by cause (lightning vs. human-caused) and see how the mix shifts. • Rank counties or regions by acres burned to find where attention should go. • Show fire activity over time, by month or by season, to spot the peak and plan for it. • Roll a region's fires into an at-a-glance snapshot: count, total acres, top causes, most recent activity, the kind of summary an official would want before a meeting. Keep your framing constructive (awareness, preparedness, helping people stay safe) and don't build anything that points a finger at named individuals for causing a fire.
